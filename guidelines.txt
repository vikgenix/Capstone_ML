== SECTION: Diabetes Overview ==
Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disease characterized by elevated blood glucose (hyperglycemia). There are two main types: Type 1 diabetes (autoimmune, insulin-dependent) and Type 2 diabetes (insulin resistance, lifestyle-related). Prediabetes is a condition where blood glucose is higher than normal but not high enough to be classified as Type 2 diabetes. Early detection and intervention are critical.

Source: American Diabetes Association (ADA), Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes, 2023.

== SECTION: Diagnostic Criteria ==
The following values, confirmed on two separate tests, indicate diabetes:
- Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG) >= 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L)
- 2-hour Plasma Glucose >= 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT)
- HbA1c >= 6.5% (48 mmol/mol)
- Random Plasma Glucose >= 200 mg/dL in a patient with classic symptoms of hyperglycemia

Prediabetes criteria:
- FPG 100–125 mg/dL (Impaired Fasting Glucose)
- 2-hour PG 140–199 mg/dL during OGTT (Impaired Glucose Tolerance)
- HbA1c 5.7–6.4%

Source: WHO, 2006. Definition and Diagnosis of Diabetes Mellitus and Intermediate Hyperglycemia.

== SECTION: Risk Factors for Type 2 Diabetes ==
Major risk factors include:
- Overweight (BMI >= 25 kg/m²) or Obesity (BMI >= 30 kg/m²)
- Physical inactivity (less than 150 minutes/week of moderate activity)
- Family history of diabetes in a first-degree relative
- Hypertension (blood pressure >= 140/90 mmHg)
- Dyslipidemia: HDL cholesterol < 35 mg/dL or triglycerides > 250 mg/dL
- History of cardiovascular disease
- History of gestational diabetes or delivery of a baby over 9 lbs
- Impaired fasting glucose or impaired glucose tolerance previously identified
- Age >= 45 years (risk increases substantially)
- Waist circumference > 40 inches (men) or >35 inches (women) indicating central obesity
- High alcohol consumption (> 14 units/week for men, > 7 units/week for women)
- Smoking: increases insulin resistance and risk by approximately 30–40%

Source: CDC, National Diabetes Prevention Program. ADA 2023.

== SECTION: Prevention Strategies ==
Lifestyle interventions can reduce the risk of Type 2 Diabetes by up to 58%:
1. Achieve and maintain a healthy body weight. Even a 5–7% reduction in body weight is proven to have a significant impact.
2. Engage in at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week (e.g., brisk walking, cycling).
3. Include resistance training (e.g., weightlifting, bodyweight exercises) at least 2–3 times per week.
4. Follow a balanced diet: rich in vegetables, fruits, whole grains, lean proteins, and healthy fats. Minimize intake of processed foods, refined carbohydrates, and sugary beverages.
5. Quit smoking. Smoking cessation immediately begins to lower the risk of diabetes and cardiovascular disease.
6. Limit alcohol consumption. Moderate drinking (up to 1 drink/day for women, 2 for men) is acceptable. Excessive use is strongly discouraged.
7. Ensure 7–9 hours of quality sleep per night. Poor sleep is associated with insulin resistance.
8. Manage stress effectively. Chronic stress elevates cortisol levels, contributing to blood sugar instability.

Source: CDC Diabetes Prevention Program. Knowler WC et al., NEJM 2002.

== SECTION: Dietary Guidelines ==
Recommended dietary patterns for diabetes prevention and management:
- The Mediterranean Diet: High in fish, olive oil, vegetables, legumes, and whole grains. Associated with reduced cardiovascular risk and improved glycemic control.
- The DASH Diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension): Reduces blood pressure and blood glucose.
- Low Glycemic Index (GI) diet: Focuses on foods that cause gradual rather than rapid rises in blood sugar (e.g., oats, lentils, most vegetables).

Foods to prioritize:
- Non-starchy vegetables (broccoli, spinach, peppers)
- Whole grains (brown rice, quinoa, whole wheat bread)
- Legumes (beans, lentils, chickpeas)
- Lean proteins (chicken, fish, tofu)
- Healthy fats (avocado, nuts, olive oil)

Foods to limit:
- Refined carbohydrates and white bread
- Sugary drinks (sodas, fruit juices)
- Processed meats (sausages, bacon)
- Saturated and trans fats
- High-sodium foods

Source: ADA Nutrition Consensus Report, Diabetes Care, 2019.

== SECTION: Physical Activity Recommendations ==
The American Diabetes Association recommends:
- At least 150 minutes/week of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise.
- No more than 2 consecutive days without exercise.
- Resistance training at least 2 days per week.
- Reduce prolonged sitting. Break up sedentary time every 30 minutes with light activity.
- For people with prediabetes: physical activity is a first-line treatment recommendation.

Benefits of exercise in diabetes management:
- Improves insulin sensitivity
- Lowers blood glucose levels
- Reduces body weight
- Improves cardiovascular health
- Lowers HbA1c by approximately 0.5–1.0%

Source: ADA Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes, 2023.

== SECTION: Hypertension and Diabetes ==
Hypertension is present in approximately 70% of people with Type 2 diabetes. Combined hypertension and diabetes significantly increases the risk of cardiovascular disease, stroke, kidney failure, and retinopathy.

Blood pressure targets for adults with diabetes:
- General target: < 130/80 mmHg
- For patients with high CV risk: < 120/80 mmHg may be appropriate

Lifestyle modifications to reduce blood pressure:
- DASH diet adherence
- Weight reduction
- Reduced sodium intake (< 2,300 mg/day)
- Regular aerobic exercise
- Limiting alcohol intake
- Smoking cessation

Pharmacological treatment: ACE inhibitors and ARBs are preferred as first-line agents in people with diabetes and hypertension.

Source: ADA 2023; JNC 8 Guidelines.

== SECTION: Cholesterol Management in Diabetes ==
Dyslipidemia is common in Type 2 diabetes. The typical pattern is elevated triglycerides, low HDL cholesterol, and small, dense LDL particles, which are highly atherogenic.

Target lipid values for diabetic patients:
- LDL Cholesterol: < 100 mg/dL (< 70 mg/dL for high cardiovascular risk)
- HDL Cholesterol: > 40 mg/dL (men), > 50 mg/dL (women)
- Triglycerides: < 150 mg/dL

Statin therapy is recommended for all patients with diabetes aged 40–75 years, regardless of baseline LDL, due to elevated cardiovascular risk.

Source: ADA 2023; American Heart Association.

== SECTION: BMI and Weight Management ==
BMI Classification:
- Underweight: < 18.5 kg/m²
- Normal weight: 18.5–24.9 kg/m²
- Overweight: 25.0–29.9 kg/m²
- Obese Class I: 30.0–34.9 kg/m²
- Obese Class II: 35.0–39.9 kg/m²
- Obese Class III (Severe): >= 40 kg/m²

A BMI >= 25 significantly increases the risk of Type 2 diabetes. Central obesity (measured by waist circumference or waist-to-hip ratio) is a strong independent risk factor.
Waist-to-hip ratio thresholds for increased risk:
- Men: >= 0.90
- Women: >= 0.85

Weight loss of 5–10% of body weight can significantly improve blood glucose control and reduce cardiovascular risk factors.

Source: WHO Obesity guidelines; ADA 2023.

== SECTION: HbA1c Targets and Monitoring ==
HbA1c (glycated hemoglobin) reflects average blood glucose over the past 2–3 months. It is the primary metric for assessing long-term glycemic control.

HbA1c targets:
- Non-diabetic (normal): < 5.7%
- Prediabetes: 5.7–6.4%
- Diabetic (general target): < 7.0%
- Diabetic (less stringent, e.g., older adults, comorbidities): < 8.0%

Fasting blood glucose targets:
- Non-diabetic: 70–99 mg/dL
- Diabetic (pre-meal): 80–130 mg/dL
- Diabetic (2 hours after meals): < 180 mg/dL

Regular monitoring of HbA1c is recommended every 3 months until target is achieved, then every 6 months.

Source: ADA Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes, 2023.

== SECTION: When to Seek Medical Attention ==
Patients should consult a healthcare provider immediately if they experience:
- Fasting blood glucose consistently above 200 mg/dL
- Symptoms of hyperglycemia: excessive thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision
- Symptoms of hypoglycemia: shaking, sweating, confusion, rapid heartbeat
- HbA1c above 9%
- Signs of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA): nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, fruity breath
- Severe chest pain, shortness of breath, or stroke symptoms

Follow-up schedule for diabetic patients:
- Medical visit: every 3–6 months
- HbA1c: every 3–6 months
- Blood pressure monitoring: every visit
- Full lipid panel: annually
- Foot examination: annually
- Eye examination: annually
- Kidney function tests (eGFR, urine albumin): annually

Source: ADA Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes, 2023; CDC Diabetes Management Guidelines.

== SECTION: Smoking and Diabetes ==
Smoking is an independent risk factor for Type 2 diabetes. Current smokers have a 30–40% higher risk of developing Type 2 diabetes compared to non-smokers. Nicotine increases insulin resistance and promotes central fat accumulation.

For people with diabetes, smoking increases the risk of:
- Cardiovascular disease
- Kidney disease
- Poor glycemic control
- Peripheral artery disease and amputations

Cessation recommendations:
- Counseling combined with pharmacotherapy (varenicline, bupropion, or nicotine replacement) is the most effective approach.
- Even former smokers' risk is substantially reduced within 5 years of quitting.

Source: CDC Smoking and Diabetes Factsheet; ADA 2023.

== SECTION: Sleep and Metabolic Health ==
Sleep deprivation (< 6 hours/night) and poor sleep quality are strongly associated with:
- Insulin resistance
- Elevated cortisol and inflammatory markers
- Increased appetite and weight gain (due to dysregulation of ghrelin and leptin)
- Higher risk of Type 2 diabetes

Recommended sleep duration:
- Adults: 7–9 hours per night (CDC/AASM guidelines)
- Consistent sleep and wake times improve metabolic outcomes.

Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) is common in Type 2 diabetes and worsens glycemic control. Screening for OSA is recommended in overweight diabetic patients.

Source: CDC Sleep Guidelines; Knutson et al., AASM 2017.
